- **All sales are final.** A Day Pass is a digital good delivered

### The exceptions are about non-delivery, not about satisfaction

- The only exceptions are cases of **non-delivery**, founded on
  the outcome: what matters is whether the purchase was delivered,
  not which step failed.
- **Every recorded non-delivery enters the remedy queue.** No
  recorded non-delivery is silently lost.
- The certain instances of non-delivery are:
  - **(a)** a payment for a capped good that could not be
    delivered; and
  - **(b)** a duplicate payment that delivers nothing incremental.
- **Honour-first ordering.** Where a remedy is due, the purchase
  is honoured first if it still can be honoured. A refund is made
  only when honouring is impossible.
- **Where a refund is due, it pays the paying wallet only.** The
  refund is paid on-chain to the wallet that paid — the paying
  wallet of record — in the token amount paid, **within 7 days of
  the payment**. A refund is never sent to a different wallet,
  including at the buyer's request: the paying wallet is the only
  address a payment can be returned to. (This is the other side of
  §6: pay from a wallet you control, because that is the wallet any
  refund returns to.)

### Refund-edge disclosure — read this before you pay

An exception refund is a **public on-chain transfer from the
product's receiving wallet**, and it **visibly identifies the
paying wallet as a refunded buyer** to anyone who reads the chain.
That visibility is a property of an on-chain refund. It is stated
here plainly, before purchase, and not buried: if a refund is due
